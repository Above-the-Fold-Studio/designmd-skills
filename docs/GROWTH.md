# Growth and contribution loop

The library should grow by usefulness and evidence, not by prompt count.

## Contribution funnel

1. **Problem signal** — a repeated design failure, missing workflow, or materially weak existing skill.
2. **Proposal** — define the user/job, scope boundary, neighboring skills, expected output, and source/provenance plan.
3. **Experimental skill** — add the smallest coherent skill package with positive and negative routing fixtures.
4. **Review** — validate overlap, accessibility and safety boundaries, provenance, retry/idempotency concerns where relevant, and unsupported claims.
5. **Evidence** — record behavioral compatibility only after real bounded execution.
6. **Promotion** — raise quality status only when machine-checkable evidence satisfies the repository policy.

## What to measure

Prefer repository-native signals that do not require telemetry in the skills themselves:

- proposals opened and accepted;
- contributor count and repeat contributors;
- time from proposal to reviewed experimental skill;
- issue reports tied to concrete workflows;
- coverage of important design jobs;
- skills with current compatibility evidence;
- stale skills requiring review.

Downloads, stars, and installs may be useful ecosystem signals, but should not substitute for quality evidence.

## Review cadence

At least quarterly, review the catalog for:

- duplicated or overlapping scopes;
- missing high-frequency design workflows;
- stale external references and installer instructions;
- compatibility evidence that names obsolete agent versions;
- provenance or license changes upstream;
- skills whose guidance conflicts with current accessibility or platform practice.

## Product boundary

DesignMD may offer managed orchestration, context extraction, verification, or other paid capabilities. The public skill's core design knowledge must remain usable without those services.
