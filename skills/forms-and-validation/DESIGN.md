# Forms and Validation

## Outcome

A form that asks only for necessary information, helps users provide it, and
makes mistakes recoverable.

## Use it when

- Creating onboarding, checkout, account, search, or settings forms.
- Error messages are vague or appear far from the affected field.
- A long form needs grouping, progress, saving, or resume behavior.
- Input requirements conflict with autofill, localization, or assistive tools.

## Design approach

Treat validation as a conversation about recovery, not a punishment after
submission. The best error prevention comes from removing ambiguity, choosing
the right control, accepting realistic input, and preserving user work.
